Yoga nidra and NSDR

Used in: 10 Minute NSDR Yoga Nidra for Sleep

What it is

A guided practice done lying down: a settling breath, an intention, attention moved around the body in a fixed order, opposite sensations such as heavy and light, and a quiet return. NSDR is the modern, non-spiritual name for the same structure.

How it is thought to work

Researchers describe it as a state between waking and sleep in which attention stays lightly anchored. Imaging and sleep studies have looked at what changes in the brain and in sleep during and after practice.

Kjaer et al. 2002Kjaer TW, Bertelsen C, Piccini P, Brooks D, Alving J, Lou HC. Increased dopamine tone during meditation-induced change of consciousness. Brain Res Cogn Brain Res 2002;13(2):255-259.
Design
Within-subject PET study, two 11C-raclopride scans per participant (yoga nidra vs attending to speech). Small sample; N is not given in the abstract and the full text was paywalled.
Measured
Ventral striatal raclopride binding, a proxy for endogenous dopamine release, together with EEG theta and self-reported experience.
Finding
Raclopride binding was 7.9% lower during yoga nidra, which the authors read as a 65% rise in dopamine release. Rated depth of relaxation did not differ between conditions. This is a small mechanism study with no clinical outcome.

Design
Narrative review (no meta-analysis).
Measured
Origins and steps of the practice, and the published physiological and psychological findings on it.
Finding
The authors report that the studies they reviewed associate yoga nidra with physiological changes and lower scores for mild depression and anxiety. One study did not find these benefits in severe depression or anxiety. Narrative reviews carry no pooled effect estimate.

Finding
Most of the included trials reported better sleep parameters with yoga nidra than with the comparison condition. Most trials had moderate to high risk of bias, and adverse events were poorly reported.

Measured
Stress, anxiety and depression scores, compared with active and with no comparators.
Finding
Pooled effects favoured yoga nidra. The authors warn that low study quality and varied delivery mean these moderate-to-large effects are probably inflated.

Jacobson's method

Progressive muscle relaxation

Used in: Progressive Muscle Relaxation

What it is

Muscle groups are tensed gently for a few seconds and then released, one group at a time, while you notice the difference between tension and letting go.

How it is thought to work

Deliberately contrasting tension and release is thought to make it easier to notice and drop the muscle tension that builds up without our awareness.

Liu et al. 2020Liu K, Chen Y, Wu D, Lin R, Wang Z, Pan L. Effects of progressive muscle relaxation on anxiety and sleep quality in patients with COVID-19. Complement Ther Clin Pract 2020;39:101132.
Design
RCT, N = 51 hospitalised isolation-ward patients. Arms: 30 min of PMR a day for 5 days, or routine care.
Measured
State-trait anxiety (STAI) and sleep quality (SRSS).
Finding
The PMR group showed lower anxiety and better sleep-quality scores after the intervention than the routine-care group. This was a small, short, unblinded trial in an unusual clinical setting.

Open the paper →

Li et al. 2026Li K, Chen S, Liu Y, Yu N. The effects of progressive muscle relaxation on sleep quality in adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. Front Public Health 2026;14:1906525.
Design
Meta-analysis of 14 RCTs (957 participants), PROSPERO-registered.
Measured
Subjective sleep quality, with subgroups by cancer status and age, and a meta-regression on number of sessions.
Finding
PMR was associated with better self-rated sleep quality overall (Hedges g = -1.24), with very high heterogeneity (I² = 85.5%). The effect was not significant in adults aged 55 and over, and it was not related to the number of sessions.

Design
Meta-analysis of 31 RCTs (2,277 participants).
Measured
Sleep quality (including PSQI), anxiety and quality of life.
Finding
PMR groups showed better sleep quality (PSQI mean difference -3.79), lower anxiety and higher quality of life than controls. Heterogeneity was very high (I² of 83-94%), so the size of the effect is uncertain.

From mindfulness-based programmes

Body scan

Used in: 12 Minute Body Scan Meditation ADHD Meditation

What it is

Attention moves slowly through the body, noticing whatever sensations are there without trying to change them. It is one of the core exercises of MBSR and MBCT.

How it is thought to work

The practice trains interoception, the sense of what is happening inside the body. Studies mostly test it as part of a full programme, which matters when reading the evidence.

5 studies. Click one for its design and findings.

Fischer et al. 2017Fischer D, Messner M, Pollatos O. Improvement of interoceptive processes after an 8-week body scan intervention. Front Hum Neurosci 2017;11:452.
Supports
Body scan, body awareness

Ussher et al. 2014Ussher M, Spatz A, Copland C, Nicolaou A, Cargill A, Amini-Tabrizi N, et al. Immediate effects of a brief mindfulness-based body scan on patients with chronic pain. J Behav Med 2014;37(1):127-134.
Design
RCT, N = 55 chronic-pain outpatients. Arms: a 10-min audio body scan (n = 27) or a natural-history reading (n = 28).
Measured
Pain severity, pain-related distress, interference and mindfulness, rated immediately before and after listening.
Finding
In the clinic, the body-scan group reported lower pain-related distress and social interference than controls. At home, no outcome differed between groups. The effects were immediate only.

Goyal et al. 2014Goyal M, Singh S, Sibinga EM, Gould NF, Rowland-Seymour A, Sharma R, et al. Meditation programs for psychological stress and well-being: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA Intern Med 2014;174(3):357-368.
Design
Systematic review and meta-analysis of 47 RCTs with active controls (3,515 participants).
Measured
Anxiety, depression, stress, mood, attention, sleep, pain and weight.
Finding
Mindfulness programmes showed small improvements in anxiety, depression and pain (moderate-strength evidence). Evidence for sleep was low or insufficient, and meditation was no better than other active treatments.

Open the paper →

Khoury et al. 2015Khoury B, Sharma M, Rush SE, Fournier C. Mindfulness-based stress reduction for healthy individuals: a meta-analysis. J Psychosom Res 2015;78(6):519-528.
Design
Meta-analysis of 29 studies (n = 2,668) in healthy adults.
Measured
Stress, anxiety, depression, distress, quality of life and burnout.
Finding
MBSR was associated with moderate improvements (between-group g = 0.53), largest for stress and smallest for burnout. Heterogeneity was high, and the studies tested the full 8-week programme, not single audio sessions.

Open the paper →

Gan et al. 2022Gan R, Zhang L, Chen S. The effects of body scan meditation: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Appl Psychol Health Well Being 2022;14(3):1062-1080.
Design
Systematic review and meta-analysis of 14 RCTs (robust variance estimation).
Measured
Seven outcome categories, including mindfulness and health-related outcomes.
Finding
Body scan on its own showed only a small effect on mindfulness against passive controls (g = 0.27). The authors concluded that a standalone body scan was not effective enough to improve health outcomes, and study quality was low. This is a useful counterweight source.

Schultz's six formulas

Autogenic training

Used in: Autogenic Training Guided

What it is

Short phrases repeated silently, such as 'my arms are heavy' and 'my arms are warm', moving through heaviness, warmth, heartbeat, breath, the belly and a cool forehead.

How it is thought to work

The phrases are a form of self-suggestion aimed at the body's resting state. It has been used and studied in Europe since the 1930s.

5 studies. Click one for its design and findings.

Stetter et al. 2002Stetter F, Kupper S. Autogenic training: a meta-analysis of clinical outcome studies. Appl Psychophysiol Biofeedback 2002;27(1):45-98.

Design
Systematic review of 8 controlled trials.
Measured
Stress and anxiety outcomes, mostly the State-Trait Anxiety Inventory.
Finding
Seven of the eight trials reported less stress with autogenic training. Most were methodologically flawed, and the authors drew no firm conclusion.

Kanji et al. 2006Kanji N, White A, Ernst E. Autogenic training to reduce anxiety in nursing students: randomized controlled trial. J Adv Nurs 2006;53(6):729-735.
Design
Three-arm RCT, N = 93 nursing students: 8 weekly autogenic training sessions, laughter therapy (attention control), or no intervention.
Measured
State and trait anxiety, burnout, blood pressure and pulse, followed up to 11 months.
Finding
Immediately after treatment, the autogenic training group showed larger falls in state and trait anxiety, blood pressure and pulse than both other groups. Burnout did not differ, and the authors call the effect short-term.

Around six breaths a minute

Slow and resonance breathing

Used in: 5 Minute Breathing Exercise

What it is

Breathing slowly, with a longer breath out than in, usually close to six breaths a minute (for example in for four, out for six).

How it is thought to work

Slow breathing is associated with changes in heart rate variability, through the baroreflex and the vagus nerve. Physiologists have measured these effects in the lab for decades.

Vaschillo et al. 2006Vaschillo EG, Vaschillo B, Lehrer PM. Characteristics of resonance in heart rate variability stimulated by biofeedback. Appl Psychophysiol Biofeedback 2006;31(2):129-142.
Design
Mechanism paper drawing on prior studies, with data from 32 adults with asthma and 24 healthy adults.
Measured
Each person's resonant breathing frequency and how it relates to height, sex, age, weight and asthma.
Finding
Resonant frequency was lower in men and in taller people, was unrelated to age, weight or asthma, and stayed stable across 10 sessions. This supports using an individual rate near 6 breaths/min rather than one exact figure.

Cognitive shuffle and imagery

Imagery for falling asleep

Used in: Waking Up at 3am? Guided Meditation to Drift Back to Sleep

What it is

Gently picturing a series of simple, unrelated objects, or a calm scene, instead of following the thoughts that keep you awake.

How it is thought to work

The idea is to replace worry and problem-solving with neutral images, which resemble the drifting imagery of normal sleep onset. Direct evidence for the shuffle itself is still thin.

2 studies. Click one for its design and findings.

Harvey et al. 2002Harvey AG, Payne S. The management of unwanted pre-sleep thoughts in insomnia: distraction with imagery versus general distraction. Behav Res Ther 2002;40(3):267-277.
Design
Controlled experiment, N = 41 people with insomnia, one night under one of three instructions: imagery distraction, general distraction, or none.
Measured
Sleep onset latency, plus the frequency of and distress from pre-sleep thoughts.
Finding
The imagery-distraction group reported shorter sleep onset latency and less pre-sleep cognitive activity than the no-instruction group. General distraction did not perform worse as predicted. This is a single-night study. It tested engaging imagery, not the cognitive shuffle itself.

Open the paper →

Lemyre et al. 2020Lemyre A, Belzile F, Landry M, Bastien CH, Beaudoin LP. Pre-sleep cognitive activity in adults: a systematic review. Sleep Med Rev 2020;50:101253.
Design
Systematic review (narrative synthesis). Co-authored by Beaudoin, who developed the cognitive shuffle and declares a commercial interest in related apps.
Measured
The nature of pre-sleep thinking, its links to sleep onset latency and insomnia, and strategies that manipulate it.
Finding
The review describes normal sleep onset as rich in sensory imagery. Tested strategies (imagery, hypnosis, distraction, suppression and others) had beneficial, negligible or detrimental effects.

What the sleep research says

Relaxation, meditation and sleep

Used in: Yoga Nidra for Sleep Waking Up at 3am? Guided Meditation to Drift Back to Sleep Sleep Meditation for Night Shift Workers

What it is

Relaxation techniques are one recognised component of behavioural approaches to sleep, alongside sleep scheduling and stimulus control.

How it is thought to work

Lowering arousal at bedtime is the common thread. Guidelines treat relaxation as one part of a wider approach, not as a stand-alone answer.

4 studies. Click one for its design and findings.

Edinger et al. 2021Edinger JD, et al. Behavioral and psychological treatments for chronic insomnia disorder in adults: an AASM clinical practice guideline. J Clin Sleep Med 2021;17(2):255-262.
Supports
Relaxation therapy: conditional recommendation as a single component

Open the paper →

Ong et al. 2014Ong JC, Manber R, Segal Z, Xia Y, Shapiro S, Wyatt JK. A randomized controlled trial of mindfulness meditation for chronic insomnia. Sleep 2014;37(9):1553-1563.
Design
Three-arm RCT, N = 54 adults with chronic insomnia: MBSR, mindfulness-based therapy for insomnia (MBTI), or self-monitoring.
Measured
Total wake time (diary), pre-sleep arousal, Insomnia Severity Index, polysomnography and actigraphy.
Finding
Both meditation arms showed larger falls in total wake time, pre-sleep arousal and insomnia severity than self-monitoring. MBTI had the highest remission rate at 6 months. The sample was small and from a single site.

Black et al. 2015Black DS, O'Reilly GA, Olmstead R, Breen EC, Irwin MR. Mindfulness meditation and improvement in sleep quality and daytime impairment among older adults with sleep disturbances: a randomized clinical trial. JAMA Intern Med 2015;175(4):494-501.
Design
RCT, N = 49 older adults (mean age 66) with moderate sleep disturbance: 6 weeks of mindful awareness practices (n = 24) or sleep hygiene education (n = 25).
Measured
PSQI sleep quality, plus insomnia symptoms, depression, fatigue, anxiety, stress and NF-κB.
Finding
The mindfulness group showed a larger PSQI improvement than the sleep-hygiene group (between-group difference 1.8 points). Anxiety, stress and NF-κB did not differ between groups, and effects were measured immediately after treatment.

Rusch et al. 2019Rusch HL, Rosario M, Levison LM, Olivera A, Livingston WS, Wu T, et al. The effect of mindfulness meditation on sleep quality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. Ann N Y Acad Sci 2019;1445(1):5-16.
Design
Meta-analysis of 18 RCTs (1,654 participants).
Measured
Sleep quality against specific active controls (e.g. evidence-based sleep treatments) and non-specific active controls.
Finding
Mindfulness improved sleep quality compared with non-specific controls (ES 0.33 after treatment). It showed no difference from evidence-based sleep treatments (low-strength evidence).

Sleeping while the world is awake

Shift work and daytime sleep

Used in: Sleep Meditation for Night Shift Workers

What it is

Practices for winding down after a night shift and settling into sleep during the day, with sound that masks daytime noise.

How it is thought to work

Shift workers sleep against their body clock. Research looks at many kinds of support; relaxation specifically is barely studied in this group, which is why we keep the claims modest.

3 studies. Click one for its design and findings.

Slanger et al. 2016Slanger TE, Gross JV, Pinger A, Morfeld P, Bellinger M, Duhme AL, et al. Person-directed, non-pharmacological interventions for sleepiness at work and sleep disturbances caused by shift work. Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2016;(8):CD010641.
Design
Cochrane systematic review of 17 RCTs (556 participants): bright light, naps, and other interventions such as exercise or sleep education.
Measured
Sleepiness on shift, and sleep length and quality between shifts.
Finding
The evidence was low or very low quality and inconclusive. The authors could not determine whether these interventions improve shift workers' sleep. No relaxation trials were included.

Open the paper →

Crowther et al. 2021Crowther ME, Ferguson SA, Vincent GE, Reynolds AC. Non-pharmacological interventions to improve chronic disease risk factors and sleep in shift workers: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Clocks Sleep 2021;3(1):132-178.
Design
Systematic review of 65 publications, 39 meta-analysed.
Measured
Objective and subjective sleep duration, sleep efficiency, sleep quality, blood pressure, BMI and perceived health.
Finding
Interventions were associated with longer objective sleep (g = 0.73) and better sleep efficiency (g = 0.48). Effects on self-rated sleep duration and quality were small and their confidence intervals included zero.

Open the paper →

Jeon et al. 2023Jeon BM, Kim SH, Shin SH. Effectiveness of sleep interventions for rotating night shift workers: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Front Public Health 2023;11:1187382.
Design
Systematic review of 30 studies (RCTs and other clinical trials), 25 meta-analysed.
Measured
Sleep outcomes across pharmacological, light, cognitive-behavioural, aroma/alternative and scheduling interventions.
Finding
Interventions overall were associated with a moderate sleep benefit (g = 0.59). Pooling very different intervention types limits what this says about relaxation specifically.

Why the music is slow and soft

Music and sound

Used in: 10 Minute NSDR Progressive Muscle Relaxation 12 Minute Body Scan Meditation Autogenic Training Guided and every other practice

What it is

Every practice sits on a slow, soft music bed, with gentle rain or pads for the sleep practices.

How it is thought to work

Studies on music for sleep and stress report mixed but often positive results, mostly on how people feel rather than on objective measures.

3 studies. Click one for its design and findings.

Thoma et al. 2013Thoma MV, La Marca R, Brönnimann R, Finkel L, Ehlert U, Nater UM. The effect of music on the human stress response. PLoS One 2013;8(8):e70156.
Design
Randomised experiment, N = 60 healthy women: relaxing music, rippling-water sound, or silent rest before a standardised psychosocial stress test.
Measured
Salivary cortisol and alpha-amylase, heart rate, RSA, perceived stress and anxiety.
Finding
Alpha-amylase returned to baseline faster after music than after silent rest. However, the cortisol response was highest in the music group and lowest with water sounds, and heart rate and self-reported measures did not differ. The results are mixed.

Open the paper →

de et al. 2020de Witte M, Spruit A, van Hooren S, Moonen X, Stams GJ. Effects of music interventions on stress-related outcomes: a systematic review and two meta-analyses. Health Psychol Rev 2020;14(2):294-324.
Design
Two multilevel meta-analyses of 104 RCTs (9,617 participants, 327 effect sizes).
Measured
Physiological markers (heart rate, blood pressure, hormones) and psychological stress outcomes.
Finding
Music interventions were associated with small-to-moderate stress effects (d = 0.38 physiological, d = 0.55 psychological), and the largest physiological effect was on heart rate.

Open the paper →

Jespersen et al. 2022Jespersen KV, Pando-Naude V, Koenig J, Jennum P, Vuust P. Listening to music for insomnia in adults. Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2022;8(8):CD010459.
Design
Cochrane systematic review of 13 RCTs (1,007 participants), with daily pre-recorded music for 25-60 min over 3 days to 3 months.
Measured
PSQI sleep quality, insomnia severity, sleep onset latency, total sleep time, sleep efficiency and adverse events.
Finding
There was moderate-certainty evidence that music groups had better self-rated sleep quality (PSQI mean difference -2.79). The three studies using objective measures showed no clear improvement, and all studies had high performance bias.

Shorter silences, more anchors

Restless minds and ADHD

Used in: ADHD Meditation

What it is

Practices adapted for minds that wander: shorter silences, frequent anchors, permission to move a little.

How it is thought to work

Mindfulness programmes for adults with ADHD have been tested in trials. The effects look smaller when compared with active alternatives, so we describe it as a complement, not a replacement.

Mitchell et al. 2017Mitchell JT, McIntyre EM, English JS, Dennis MF, Beckham JC, Kollins SH. A pilot trial of mindfulness meditation training for ADHD in adulthood: impact on core symptoms, executive functioning, and emotion dysregulation. J Atten Disord 2017;21(13):1105-1120.
Design
Pilot RCT, N = 20: 8 weeks of group mindfulness (n = 11) or waitlist (n = 9).
Measured
Feasibility, ADHD and executive-function symptoms (self, clinician and EMA ratings), executive-function tasks and emotion dysregulation.
Finding
Participants reported better ADHD symptoms, executive functioning and emotion regulation than the waitlist group. Lab executive-function tasks did not improve, and the sample was very small.

Design
Multicentre, single-blind RCT, N = 120: 8 weeks of MBCT plus usual treatment (n = 60) or usual treatment alone (n = 60).
Measured
Clinician-rated ADHD symptoms (blinded), self-reports, executive functioning and mindfulness, followed to 6 months.
Finding
The MBCT group showed a larger fall in clinician-rated ADHD symptoms (d = 0.41), maintained at 6 months. MBCT was added to usual care and compared with no added activity.

Design
Preliminary RCT, N = 103 adults with ADHD: 12 weeks of adapted MBCT (n = 55) or waitlist (n = 48).
Measured
Investigator-rated and self-reported ADHD symptoms, executive functioning, depression, anxiety and mindfulness.
Finding
The MBCT group showed fewer ADHD symptoms and better executive functioning than the waitlist group. Depression, anxiety and general functioning did not improve, and a waitlist control cannot separate specific from non-specific effects.

Design
Systematic review and meta-analysis of 31 studies (children and adults, controlled and observational).
Measured
ADHD symptoms, associated conditions, neurocognition, mindfulness, functioning and quality of life.
Finding
In adults, mindfulness showed a medium pooled effect on ADHD symptoms against waitlists, but only a low or negligible effect against active controls, with some publication bias. The authors see it as a complement to other treatment, not a replacement.

The efficacy of mindfulness-based interventions in attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder beyond core symptoms: a systematic review, meta-analysis, and meta-regression. J Affect Disord 2021;292:475-486.
Design
Systematic review and meta-analysis of 31 studies (children and adults, controlled and observational).
Measured
ADHD symptoms, associated conditions, neurocognition, mindfulness, functioning and quality of life.
Finding
In adults, mindfulness showed a medium pooled effect on ADHD symptoms against waitlists, but only a low or negligible effect against active controls, with some publication bias. The authors see it as a complement to other treatment, not a replacement.

Design
Randomised experiment, N = 60 healthy women: relaxing music, rippling-water sound, or silent rest before a standardised psychosocial stress test.
Measured
Salivary cortisol and alpha-amylase, heart rate, RSA, perceived stress and anxiety.
Finding
Alpha-amylase returned to baseline faster after music than after silent rest. However, the cortisol response was highest in the music group and lowest with water sounds, and heart rate and self-reported measures did not differ. The results are mixed.
